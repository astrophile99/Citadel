# Citadel Philosophy: The Core Rules of Engagement

## 1. The Absence of Failure
In Citadel, failure is not a state. It is simply a pause. 
*   We never show "failed" dialogs, red error marks, or broken structures.
*   We do not use terms like "missed," "late," or "abandoned."
*   If a Commander does not complete today's missions, they are not scolded. The world simply transitions into a quiet, misty night, waiting for their return.

---

## 2. Permanent Progress
Progress in Citadel is a one-way street: forward. 
*   A garden that has been grown does not wither and die.
*   A tower that has been lit remains standing.
*   The Commander's hard work is permanent. 
*   What changes is the **presence of light and activity**, never the structural progress itself. When the user is away, the lights dim and mist rolls in. When they return, they are waking up their built progress, not rebuild it from rubble.

---

## 3. The Graceful Return
Typical productivity apps punish absence by greeting the user with a giant stack of overdue tasks and notifications about their broken streak. Citadel does the opposite:
*   **The Clean Slate**: Overdue tasks from previous days are automatically moved to the "Chronicle Archives" where they rest in peace without negative marks.
*   **The Warm Welcome**: The Companion greets the Commander back with warmth, focusing on today rather than highlighting what was missed.
*   **Healing the Land**: Waking the Citadel requires completing just a single task, which immediately clears the fog and sparks the campfire.

---

## 4. Emotional Attachment over Dopamine Loops
We reject traditional gamification mechanics that build addictive hooks:
*   No slot-machine sound effects.
*   No flashing lights, gem counters, or experience bars.
*   Instead, we build attachment. The player cares about the Citadel because it acts as a peaceful, living reflection of their real-life efforts.
