# Citadel Player Persona: The Commander's Journey

This document defines the ideal user of Citadel, focusing on their psychological profile, emotional struggles, and long-term experience journey.

---

## 1. The Ideal User Profile

### Why They Install Citadel
*   They are exhausted by typical habit trackers that use flashing alerts, pressure streaks, and gamified leveling up.
*   They want to bring order to their personal life or daily habits but feel overwhelmed by traditional corporate-style productivity lists.
*   They respond to cozy, narrative, and atmospheric media (e.g., *Journey*, *Monument Valley*, quiet indie games).

### What They Struggle With
*   **Anxiety & Guilt**: They experience task paralysis. If they miss a single day, they feel like they have failed completely, leading to habit abandonment.
*   **Cognitive Overload**: A busy list of 20 tasks makes them close the app out of stress.
*   **Screen Fatigue**: They want to spend less time staring at their phones and more time living.

---

## 2. The Timeline Experience

*   **Day 1 (Intrigue & Relief)**:
    The Commander opens the app and is greeted by a quiet obsidian silhouette and a serene campfire. They add three simple daily tasks. When they close the app, there are no persistent red notification badges.
*   **Week 1 (Habit Trust)**:
    The Commander misses a day due to real-life obligations. Opening the app the next day, they expect to see an "overdue" alert. Instead, they find a quiet mist covering the valley, and the Companion welcomes them back with a warm cup of tea. They realize they are safe here.
*   **Month 1 (Familiar Home)**:
    The Commander starts recognizing how the Citadel changes. They notice a couple of white flowers blooming in the courtyard and a campfire that burns brighter. The app has become a comforting, daily morning/evening ritual.
*   **Year 1 (Emotional Anchorage)**:
    The Citadel has quietly evolved through seasons of snow and autumn leaves. It acts as a historical diary of their year—a visual sanctuary reflecting their steady, self-compassionate consistency.

---

## 3. Who is NOT Our Target Audience

*   **The Power-Optimizer**: Users who want complex spreadsheets, Pomodoro timers, subtasks, color-coded tag tags, and multi-device sync.
*   **The Sensation Seeker**: Users who want flashy animations, sound effects, treasure chests, and competitive leaderboards.
*   **The Passive Organizer**: Users who want an app that automates everything rather than participating in a manual daily planning ritual.
